Core Mechanics

Core Resolution

  • Roll d20 + STAT modifier vs DR (Difficulty Rating)
  • If the result is equal to or higher than DR → success
  • Default DR is 12, but it can shift based on the situation

Difficulty Rating (DR)

When a player attempts something risky, the GM sets a Difficulty Rating (DR) for the task. The player rolls 1d20 + STAT modifier and compares it to the DR. If the total equals or exceeds the DR, the action succeeds.

DR values represent how hard something is to accomplish:

  • 6 – Trivial
  • 8 – Routine
  • 10 – Skilled
  • 12 – Pressured
  • 14 – Tough
  • 16 – Heroic
  • 18+ – Impossible?

Most checks are DR12 unless the situation calls for something easier or harder. Let the fiction guide the number.

Stats

Each character has four stats:

  • STR – Strength: melee, brute force, lifting
  • AGI – Agility: dodging, stealth, defense
  • PRE – Presence: awareness, ranged attacks, magic
  • TOU – Toughness: survival, resistance, HP bonus

Roll 3d6 for each stat. Use the total to determine:

  • Your stat bonus or modifiers (used in most rolls)
  • Your passive chance of success ( d6 behind-the-screen rolls by the GM)
  • Your spells per day (only for characters who can cast)
3d6 Roll STAT Bonus d6 chances (GM rolls behind screen) Spells per Day
3–5 -1 0-in-6 —
6–10 0 1-in-6 + d2 per day
11–14 +1 2-in-6 + d3 per day
15–17 +2 3-in-6 + d4 per day
18 +3 4-in-6 + d6 per day

Sometimes, the GM will roll in secret to determine if a player notices or reacts to something without declaring an action, like spotting a trap, hearing footsteps, sensing danger, or intuiting a lie. Use the player's stat to determine their passive success chance on a d6.

Active vs Passive Abilities

  • If a player chooses to do something, they roll a standard test:
    d20 + STAT vs DR (usually DR12)
  • If a player is unaware or not actively trying, the GM uses the passive d6 chance.

Examples of Passive Use:

  • AGI: Detecting a tripwire, reflexively dodging a sudden hazard
  • PRE: Hearing whispers behind a door, noticing someone's lying
  • TOU: Resisting fatigue, poison, or mind-altering fog
  • STR: Bracing against sudden collapse or holding a door shut

The GM rolls the d6 behind the screen and only informs the player if something is noticed, resisted, or if it’s too late. If preferred, this roll can be made openly, either by the GM or by the player in front of everyone.

Advantage & Disadvantage

  • Advantage: Roll d20 + STAT Modifier +2
  • Disadvantage: Roll d20 + STAT Modifier -2

The GM applies these based on narrative positioning, tools, or environment.

MAX Health

Starting MAX HP = 1d6 + TOU

Death & Broken

If HP drops below zero, roll d20 + TOU vs DR15:

If HP drops to 0, roll d20 + TOU vs DR12:

  • Success = Broken (roll on the Broken Table)
  • Failure = Dead

When Broken, roll a d6 to determine outcome (e.g., unconscious, wounded, or bleeding out). All rolls become DR16 until treated.

Encumbrance

  • Carrying limit = 8 + STR slots
  • Bulky items = 2 slots
  • Over capacity? Move limited to Close range and all AGI/DEF rolls are made at DR +2

DethKrawler’s Die

You start with Level + 1 DethKrawler Die ( d6). You may spend one to:

  • Add to a Test or Damage roll
  • Block d6 incoming damage

Cannot be used on a natural 1.
You may gift one die to another player.

Movement & Ranges

  • Ranges are abstract: Close, Mid, Far, Out of Range
  • You may move one range band per turn unless hindered.

Close

(Melee, 5 ft)

You are within 5 feet of a creature, close enough to take a step and make a melee attack. If you are within 5 feet of another creature, both of you are considered engaged in melee and may target each other with melee attacks or opportunity attacks.

Mid

(Reach/Thrown/Spell Range, ~15 ft or as specified)

You are within the effective range of a reach weapon (such as a glaive or whip), thrown weapon, or short-range spell. Attacks follow normal range rules. For ranged weapons and spells with longer ranges, attacks beyond the normal range (but within maximum range) are made at disadvantage.

Far

(Ranged Line of Sight, ~60 ft or greater)

You are far enough that you can hear combat (a Perception check may be required), but you cannot currently see or target any creatures involved. You must move to gain line of sight or a usable range to interact.

Out of Range

(Beyond ~120 ft / Unaware)

You are so far away that you are unaware the combat is occurring — you cannot hear, see, or otherwise sense the fight, and cannot interact with it in any way until informed or moved closer.

Recovery

Short Rest: Eat and drink → regain d4 HP
Long Rest: Eat, drink, and rest undisturbed → regain d6 HP

No food or water = no HP recovery
After 2 days without sustenance = lose d4 HP per day

Spell Casting

When a player chooses to play a spellcaster, their PRE modifier determines how many spells they can cast each day (see the Stat Table). This is their spells per day limit.

When PC rolls for spellcasting, and they are using a source with spell levels, each spell’s difficulty rating increases with its level: Level 1 spells are DR12, Level 2 spells are DR13, and each higher level adds +1 DR.

Spell Dice Resolution

  • Roll d20 + PRE vs DR (Difficulty Rating)
  • If the result is equal to or higher than DR → success
  • Spellcasting DR is 12, but it can shift based on the situation.

When casting a spell, the player cannot use a DethKrawler Die if they roll a natural 1. In this case, the GM will roll on the Mishap Table and narrate the outcome.

Corruption

Whenever a player commits an evil deed or rolls a natural 1 when spellcasting, they gain 1 point of Corruption. When a player character reaches 10 Corruption points, the GM takes control of the character and may use them as a monster in the game.

Critical Miscast

On a natural 1 when spellcasting, the caster gains +1 Corruption and must roll d20 on the following table:

Roll Effect
1–5 Fizzle
Spell does not work. Ghostly laughter fills the room.
6–10 Inconvenience
The caster’s nose bleeds uncontrollably, their hair turns white or falls out, boils appear on their skin, or they forget the spell for the rest of the day.
11–15 Backfire
The caster takes the damage or effect, or the opposite of the intended spell occurs (e.g., a shield spell lowers the caster’s armor class).
16 Friendly Fire

17 Armor Melt

The caster gains a mutation (third eye, tentacle, conjoined twin, etc.).

The caster explodes in a shower of gore, or a portal to hell opens and they are dragged away by demons.

How Many Spells Can I Learn?

In addition to your daily casting limit (based on PRE), you may learn and retain a number of spells equal to your PRE stat (minimum 1) plus your level. This removes the need for complex spell slot tracking and keeps bookkeeping simple. After character creation, the GM may assign spells, generate them randomly, or let you select them from any OSR or 5E source.

How Can I Learn New Spells?

Knowledge is dangerous. The mind can only hold so many whispers of the arcane without shattering. In addition to your daily casting limit (based on PRE), you may learn and retain a number of spells equal to your PRE stat (minimum 1) plus your level. This removes the need for complex spell slot tracking and keeps bookkeeping simple. After character creation, the GM may assign spells, generate them randomly, or let you select them from any OSR or 5E source.

Wizards trust no one. Most hide among villages or within the ruins of fallen towers, jealously guarding their knowledge. To convince such a wizard to teach you a spell, you must succeed on a PRE test against DR18. Even then, payment is steep and the price may be more than coin. The wise fear the true cost of wielding the arcane.
