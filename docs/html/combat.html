Combat

Surprise Roll

Before combat or a tense encounter, the GM and the scouting player each roll a d6.

  • If the player rolls higher, they are not surprised and may act normally.
  • If the GM rolls higher, the player is surprised, they cannot act during the first round.
  • On a tie, both parties act cautiously, no one is truly surprised, but initiative proceeds as normal.

If no player is scouting or keeping watch, the GM rolls only, and surprise is automatic unless the players succeed on an Active PRE test roll.

Initiative

Dethkrawl is about fast combat. GM can decide whether to use a 1d6 roll to determine which group goes first in a PC vs. GM roll. The person who rolls the highest will go first. Remember GM will ask for declarations before rolling for initiative.

Quicker Combat

Before asking for player declarations at the beginning of the round, the GM rolls 1dY, where Y is the number of players at the table.

This determines which players the enemies will target during the round.

Once targets are set, each targeted player rolls 2d20:

  • One die is the ATTACK roll
  • The other die is the DEFENSE roll

Resolve both rolls in the same beat. The GM narrates what happens based on both rolls, blending action and reaction into a single cinematic moment.

Quick Combat Example:

The GM is running combat for 4 players, so they roll 4d4 one die per enemy. The results are: 3, 3, 1, 4.

  • Two enemies target Player 3
  • One enemy targets Player 1
  • One enemy targets Player 4

Aftermath

The GM narrates how the enemies advance — some charging with blades drawn, others firing from a distance.
The GM then prompts Player 3 to roll their ATTACK and DEFENSE dice.

Player 3 rolls:

  • 4 on the ATTACK die
  • 8 on the DEFENSE die

Since both rolls are below the standard DR12, the attack misses, and the player is hit by the enemy’s assault. The GM rolls damage, then PC can roll armor reduction as needed.

Optional: Alternative Armor Play


Player may use Ascending Armor Class instead of using tier armor and rolling dice against incoming damage. Making this way of playing more streamline for those who come from Old School Renaissance or 5e players.

Armor Conversion

Tier Armor VS AC

  • Unarmored = AC 12
  • -d2/Tier 1 = AC 14
  • -d4/Tier 2 = AC 16
  • -d6/Tier 3= AC 18

If playing with AC mode, no need to roll damage reduction. The idea of AC is how difficult is to hit you. This form of play, GM can roll for enemies against the PC's AC. Roll straight 1d20.

Remove the Damage Reduction Roll for Tier Armor
  • Unarmored = takes fulll damage
  • -d2/Tier 1 = -1 damage
  • -d4/Tier 2 = -2 damage
  • -d6/Tier 3 = -3 damage
