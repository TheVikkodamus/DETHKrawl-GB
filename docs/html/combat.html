Combat

Surprise Roll

Before combat or a tense encounter, the GM and the scouting player each roll a d6.

  • If the player rolls higher, they are not surprised and may act normally.
  • If the GM rolls higher, the player is surprised, they cannot act during the first round.
  • On a tie, both parties act cautiously, no one is truly surprised, but initiative proceeds as normal.

If no player is scouting or keeping watch, the GM rolls only, and surprise is automatic unless the players succeed on an Active PRE test roll.

Initiative

Dethkrawl is about fast combat. GM can decide whether to use a 1d6 roll to determine which group goes first in a PC vs. GM roll. The person who rolls the highest will go first. Remember Game Master (GM) will ask for declarations before rolling for initiative.

Quicker Combat

Step 1: Target Assignment

At the start of combat, the GM rolls 1dY where Y is the number of players at the table. This determines which player each enemy targets.

For example, if there are 4 players and 4 enemies, the GM rolls 4d4. The result might be: 3, 3, 1, 4 — meaning:

  • Two enemies target Player 3
  • One enemy targets Player 1
  • One enemy targets Player 4
Step 2: Player Rolls

Each targeted player rolls 2d20:

  • One for ATTACK
  • One for DEFENSE

Resolve both results in a single beat of action. The GM narrates what happens based on the outcome of both rolls — blending offense and defense into a cinematic moment.

Defense Difficulty:
Base DR is 12. Increase by +1 for each additional enemy targeting the same player.

Step 3: Untargeted Players

Players who are not targeted by enemies this round may act freely:

  • Make a ranged or melee attack
  • Move into combat or reposition
  • Use an item, cast a spell, or help another player
Step 4: Resolve Damage

If a player fails their DEFENSE roll, they are hit. The GM rolls damage using the highest damage die among the attacking enemies, plus +1 for each extra enemy beyond the first.

The player may then roll for armor reduction, if wearing armor.

Disengaging & Movement

If a player or enemy attempts to move away from a target they are engaged with in melee, they must succeed on an AGILITY test (usually DR12) to avoid a free attack of opportunity.

End of Round

After all actions have resolved, the GM asks for declarations for the next round. Repeat the process.

Combat Result Breakdown

Attack Roll Defense Roll Outcome
✓ ✓ You hit and avoid being hit
✓ ✗ Both you and the enemy hit
✗ ✓ You miss, but avoid being hit
✗ ✗ You miss, and are hit

Optional: Alternative Armor Play

Player may use Ascending Armor Class instead of using tier armor and rolling dice against incoming damage. Making this way of playing more streamline for those who come from Old School Renaissance or 5e players.

Armor Conversion

Tier Armor VS AC

  • Unarmored = AC 12
  • -d2/Tier 1 = AC 14
  • -d4/Tier 2 = AC 16
  • -d6/Tier 3= AC 18

If playing with AC mode, no need to roll damage reduction. The idea of AC is how difficult is to hit you. This form of play, GM can roll for enemies against the PC's AC. Roll straight 1d20.
